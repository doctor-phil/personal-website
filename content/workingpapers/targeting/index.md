<li><b>Abstract:</b></li>
<ul>
   Optimal targeting is determined not by who is most central or whose opinions are most extreme, but by which disagreement persists and who can move it. We study intervention in an opinion network when a decision-maker commits to one exposure rule and adjusts only its intensity. Reuse makes targeting an endogenous-path problem: the rule shapes the opinion path on which it will later be used and must therefore be chosen against the path it creates. Under equal centrality, uniform exposure is optimal exactly when updating erases inherited disagreement; otherwise exposure tilts toward disagreement the network carries forward. When centrality differs, exposure also follows agents' propagated leverage. The value of retargeting is governed by the effective dimension of the realized path: a fixed rule can match full targeting when it reproduces unrestricted intervention along that path. Combining Yale climate-opinion data with the Facebook friendship network, we find that California has substantially more authority than Texas but receives nearly identical exposure because Texas has greater discounted disagreement. In this application, the realized path remains nearly one-dimensional even after removing the common opinion component, so a single fixed rule captures almost all attainable gains.
</ul>
